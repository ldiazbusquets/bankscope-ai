What this model leaves out

New borrowing from the Federal Home Loan Banks or the Federal Reserve (which would let a bank pledge securities instead of selling them), insured depositors leaving, taxes, changes in risk-weighted assets, and anything management would do in response. Regulatory ratios after the shock are approximations that assume unrealized AFS losses were excluded from capital. This is a sensitivity exercise, not a supervisory stress test.

How the data gets here

  1. Rank. Each build asks the FDIC for the latest reporting quarter and ranks U.S.-chartered insured banks by total assets. The 50 largest are included, plus three banks that failed in 2023. U.S. branches of foreign banks are left out because they file no equity capital.
  2. Collect. For each bank, GitHub Actions downloads up to twelve quarters from the BankFind /financials endpoint, and names, locations and regulators from /institutions.
  3. Calculate. Python computes derived ratios, screening checks and the baseline stress case. Missing fields stay blank rather than being filled with zero. The browser's interactive stress test runs the same formula, and an automated test checks that both give identical answers.
  4. Publish. Results are written as JSON and deployed to GitHub Pages. The browser recalculates only the interactive stress scenarios.

Reading the results

Each figure belongs to an individual FDIC-insured bank charter. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. is not the same reporting entity as JPMorgan Chase & Co., its holding company, and some large companies own several charters.

Dollar amounts are reported to the FDIC in thousands and displayed here in millions, billions or trillions. Return on assets, return on equity and net interest margin are year-to-date figures annualized by the FDIC.

Equity / assets is an accounting measure. It is related to, but different from, the regulatory Tier 1 leverage and risk-based capital ratios.

Failed banks show their last report before closing, so their dates are older than the rest of the list.
